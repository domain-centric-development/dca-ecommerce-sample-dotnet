# Is the order placed at the checkout's captured price, against the shared vector that refuses it — and where does the review scenario, which already holds, go?

## Question
Two findings, one answer decides both.

1. `the-order-is-placed-at-the-checkouts-price` asks that "Place Order" after a price change confirms the order at the
   captured price. Today `CheckoutPricing.ValidateItems` reports a changed price
   (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Domain/Service/CheckoutPricing.cs:36`), `ConfirmCheckoutUseCase` refuses on it
   (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Application/CheckoutCompletion/ConfirmCheckout/ConfirmCheckoutUseCase.cs:60`), and the
   review shows "Cannot place the order, 1 item(s) unavailable or out of stock."
   (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Adapter/Incoming/Web/CheckoutStepNotice.cs:69`, left at
   `src/DcaShop.Checkout/Adapter/Incoming/Web/CheckoutCompletion/CheckoutStepPage.cs:203`). That refusal is agreed
   behaviour the story does not name under a `## Changed expectations`: the shared specification's vector
   `checkout.confirm.price-changed` (`accept: false`, `errors: 1`), asserted by
   `tests/DcaShop.UnitTests/Specification/CheckoutSpecificationTest.cs:64-76` (runs with `-p:SpecificationPath`);
   ADR-012 (`docs/architecture/adr/adr-012-checkout-snapshots-and-reconciliation.md:18-20`: "Any changed price …
   leaves state, totals and events unchanged. The buyer explicitly starts a fresh checkout against the new prices");
   the glossary (`product/spec/checkout/glossary.md:48-51` and the `Confirm` line at 65); `README.md:361` and
   `AGENTS.md:271` repeat it. The answered Q4.7 (`planning/questions-2026-10-15.md:77`, "open checkout keeps its
   snapshot") says what the customer sees, not whether the order is placed at it. The specification's semantics are
   the person's, and both shops change their adapters, schemas and glossaries together.
2. `an-open-checkout-keeps-the-price-it-started-with` (happy path) holds today: the review renders
   `CheckoutCartSnapshot.From(session)` (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Application/Session/GetCheckoutSession/GetCheckoutSessionUseCase.cs:23`),
   whose lines carry the captured `UnitPrice` and whose totals are the session's
   (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Domain/ReadModel/CheckoutCartSnapshot.cs:31,41-42`); no checkout page reads the current price
   (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Adapter/Incoming/Web/CheckoutCompletion/ReviewPageController.cs:32`). No story and no test
   asserts it under a price change (the nearest, `tests/DcaShop.E2eTests/OpenCheckoutKeepsItsOrderE2eTest.cs:15`, is
   about cart edits). The test gate refuses a green test, so the scenario cannot stay in a story that builds.

## Options
- a: The order is placed at the captured price. The vector `checkout.confirm.price-changed` turns to `accept: true`,
  `total: "20.00"` in `dca-sample-specification/vectors/checkout.json` and both shops follow —
  `CheckoutSpecificationTest` moves that id to the accepting case (changes a test) — and ADR-012, the glossary, the
  README and AGENTS.md say that a changed price does not refuse the order (document stage). In this story the review
  scenario leaves for an adopted story of its own (characterization test with a break) and
  `the-order-is-placed-at-the-checkouts-price` takes the `(happy path)` mark; the Java twin's copy gets the same shape.
- b: The agreed semantics stay: a changed price refuses "Place Order" and the guest starts a fresh checkout. The story
  becomes `adopted` with its second scenario rewritten to the refusal as the review shows it today; the review
  scenario stays; the vector stays. The refusal's wording, which speaks of stock for a price, is a story of its own.
- c: The story is superseded: neither scenario is wanted as a spec line now; the vector, the ADR and the review's
  behaviour stay as they are, and the refusal's wording is a finding.

## Recommendation
a — the story line says "the price I am reviewing to be the price I order at"; a review that shows one price and then
refuses to order at it says the opposite, and the fresh-checkout rule was written (2026-09-09) when nothing outside the
tests could change a price. The cost is one vector and the documents that repeat it, in both shops.
