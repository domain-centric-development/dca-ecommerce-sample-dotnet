# Two "sent too early" scenarios describe a redirect the shop does not make — reword them, build it, or take them out?

## Question

The story is `adopted`, so every scenario must already hold. Two do only in part:

- `delivery-sent-before-the-buyer-step` — *Then the guest ends on the buyer step at `/checkout/buyer`.* Today the
  session refuses the delivery (`CheckoutStepNotCompletedException`, `src/DcaShop.Checkout/Domain/Model/CheckoutSession.cs:91`),
  and the controller answers the POST with `200`, re-rendering the **delivery** page with
  "Please complete the previous checkout steps first." (`src/DcaShop.Checkout/Adapter/Incoming/Web/CheckoutPageController.cs:166`, `:206`).
  No redirect to `/checkout/buyer`. The `And` (review afterwards opens the buyer step) holds.
- `place-order-before-the-review` — *Then the guest ends on the payment step at `/checkout/payment`.* Today
  `POST /checkout/confirm` from the payment step is refused (`CheckoutSession.cs:149`) and answered with `200`,
  re-rendering the **review** page with "Please complete the previous checkout steps first." (`CheckoutPageController.cs:203`).
  The `And` (no confirmation, order not placed) holds.

The rule both stand under — "A step's form sent before the earlier steps are done changes nothing" — holds today.

## Options

1. **Reword the two `Then`s to what the shop does** and keep the story adopted: "Then the delivery page answers
   'Please complete the previous checkout steps first.'" / "Then the review page answers 'Please complete the
   previous checkout steps first.'"; the `And`s stay.
2. **Build the redirect**: take the two scenarios out of this adopted story into a new buildable story whose
   refused premature submit redirects to the current step (`StepAccess` already names it). A change to the Java
   sample's twin behaviour too, to stay in step.
3. **Take the two scenarios out** of this story; the rule is then carried by no end-user scenario.

## Recommendation

Option 1. The rule's substance — nothing changes, the order is not placed — holds and is what matters; the page
the guest lands on is a presentation detail, and a re-rendered step with a named reason is consistent with how
every other refused submit is answered (`CheckoutPageController.cs:162`). Option 2 if the replay of `CHK-06`
must match the Java sample's redirect word for word — check the Java sample first.

## Answer
answer: 2, as a change story in this shop only — the Java shop holds the story (a refused step redirects, and the step guard sends the guest to the step that is open). The .NET shop is brought in line by early-step-leads-to-the-open-step, which this story depends on. This story stays adopted as written.
