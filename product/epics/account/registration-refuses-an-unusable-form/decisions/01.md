# Four scenarios expect a sentence the registration page does not show. Change the story, or deliver the page wording?

## Question
The story is `status: adopted`, which means it describes behaviour the shop already has. Three scenarios match the
page today. Four do not. The shop does refuse each of those four values, but the page shows its one generic sentence
instead of the scenario's text:

| Scenario | Story expects | Page shows today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| future-date-is-refused | Date of birth cannot be in the future | Please check the email address, your name and your date of birth. |
| malformed-email-is-refused | Invalid email format: anna.schmidt@example | (same) |
| blank-first-name-is-refused | First name is required | (same) |
| overlong-last-name-is-refused | Last name is too long | (same) |

Evidence:
- `src/DcaShop.Account/Adapter/Incoming/Web/RegisterPageController.cs:88-105` maps every argument exception to the
  generic sentence, and its summary says "the exception's message is for the log, never for the page".
- The story's four sentences are exception messages that are only written to the log:
  `src/DcaShop.Account/Domain/Model/Email.cs:27`, `src/DcaShop.Account/Domain/Model/Owner.cs:55` and `:61`, and
  `src/DcaShop.Account/Domain/Specification/UsableDateOfBirth.cs:36`.
- `docs/architecture/refusal-wording.md:74` (Register, argument exception) fixes the generic sentence for both shops.
- These match today and can be adopted: differing-passwords-are-refused (`RegisterPageController.cs:55-58`),
  short-password-is-refused (`:60-63`) and unreadable-date-is-refused (`:65-68`, `SubmittedDate.cs:11`).

An adoption cannot change production code, and a plan cannot rewrite the story's expected text. That is why the
plan stage has stopped.

## Options
- a: Change the four scenarios to expect "Please check the email address, your name and your date of birth." The
  story stays adopted, nothing in production changes, and each scenario gets a characterization test.
- b: Make it a story to build (no longer `adopted`). The page then words each refusal itself, from the failure's
  type and the field it concerns. This changes `docs/architecture/refusal-wording.md` in both shops, the Java
  sample's register page, and the shared scenarios. It also contradicts the "never the exception's message" rule
  as long as the sentences come from those messages.
- c: Take the four scenarios out of this story. Adopt the three that match now, and write the per-field wording as
  a new story to build later.

## Recommendation
Option a. The documented, shared wording is the generic sentence (`refusal-wording.md:74`). A different
per-field wording is a product change for both shops, and it should get a story of its own.

## Answer
answer: 1 — the four scenarios expect the page's sentence for an argument exception, "Please check the email address, your name and your date of birth.", and the story's sentence says the page names what to check, not which entry. It stays adopted; the story text is already changed, in both shops alike. Naming the field is not part of this story.
